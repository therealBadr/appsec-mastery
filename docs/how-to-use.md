# How to use this site

This material is built for people who want to learn application security by building things, not by reading summaries.

## The loop for every subject

1. **Read the subject.** Each chapter has a [subject page](roadmap.md) with the mandatory part, the forbidden list and the defense questions. Read all of it before touching a keyboard.
2. **Write down what you do not understand.** Collect keywords from the subject. Look each one up in primary sources: RFCs, man pages, official docs, source code.
3. **Read the lessons.** Where I have published a lesson on a topic, read it after you have tried to understand the keywords yourself, not before. It is a second opinion, not a substitute for the struggle.
4. **Build it in your own repository.** Not in a fork of this one. Your commit history is evidence that you did the work.
5. **Capture evidence.** For every claim your solution makes ("sshd refuses passwords"), keep the command and the output that proves it, ideally from an attack you ran yourself.
6. **Answer the defense questions from memory,** out loud or on paper, without looking anything up. If you cannot, you do not own the material yet.
7. **Only now compare with my solution.** Differences are where you learn the most. If mine is wrong, open an issue.

!!! warning "Do not skip step 4"
    The pattern that fails is reading the solution, nodding, and moving on. Understanding something you read is much weaker
    than being able to produce it. Struggle first, compare second.

## Using the weekly routine

The [weekly study routine](weekly-routine.md) splits a week into theory, lab, practice (CTFs and platforms), writing and review. Adapt the hours to your life; keep the proportions.

## What "full depth" means

The Stage 0 to 2 subjects are written to the standard of "you could explain this to a colleague, line by line, while they interrupt". Later stages are structured specs that I will deepen as I reach them. The [roadmap](roadmap.md) says which is which.

## Using AI while you study

AI is a strong teacher and a poor substitute for effort. The rules I follow, plus a prompt that keeps an AI in tutor mode, are in [Using AI as a tutor](ai-tutor.md).

## Lab and safety

Everything is practiced on machines you own, or on environments built for it. Read [Responsible use](ethics.md) once before you start.

## Found a mistake?

Open an issue from any chapter or from the repository. I credit people who correct me.
